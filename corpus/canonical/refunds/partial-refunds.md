# Partial Refunds and Deductions

## Purpose and scope

Partial refunds are used when the customer is owed money but not the full original item price. This guide explains the allowed reasons for deductions and the information that must be shown to the customer.

## Standard rule

A partial refund may be issued when returned merchandise is missing customer removable parts, shows customer caused damage, or only part of a multi item order is being refunded. The deduction must be tied to a documented reason and the customer must be told the amount before the refund is finalized.

## Conditions and decision points

- Shipping fees are refunded only when the shipping service itself failed or the return was caused by BrightCart.
- Tax is recalculated based on the refunded merchandise amount.
- Agents cannot invent a restocking fee when no published fee applies.

## Worked example

A customer returns a $120 blender but does not return the $20 travel cup that was included in the package. After the warehouse confirms the missing accessory, the refund can be reduced by the documented replacement value of the cup.

## Exceptions and edge cases

- Do not deduct for packaging that was opened as part of a normal inspection.
- Do not deduct for damage that was already reported as carrier damage.
- Any proposed deduction above $100 requires a supervisor review.

## Operational notes

The case note should identify the original price, deduction reason, deduction amount, tax adjustment, and final refund. The customer should be able to understand the calculation without contacting finance.
